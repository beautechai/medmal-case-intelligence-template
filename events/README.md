# events/: intimidation and conflict-of-interest log

Use this log for interactions that may matter later, for example:
- a hospital representative **misidentifying their role** (e.g., presenting as a "patient advocate" while working for risk management, legal or the insurer);
- **threats** about recordings, complaints, reviews, or "HIPAA violations";
- pressure to sign documents, waivers or releases, or to stop asking questions;
- retaliation after a complaint or grievance (changes in care, discharge pressure, access restrictions);
- conflicts of interest (e.g., the same person handling your grievance and the facility's defense);
- delays or obstruction of records requests.

Write entries **as soon as possible after the event**: date, time, place, who (by role), exact words as best you recall, witnesses, and any documents. Stick to facts; put interpretation in `notes`. Template: `events_TEMPLATE.csv` · Example (fictional): `events_EXAMPLE.csv`.

## Know the rules (not legal advice)

**HIPAA binds covered entities, not patients.** The HIPAA Rules apply to *covered entities* (health plans, clearinghouses, and health care providers that conduct standard electronic transactions) and their *business associates*. "If an entity does not meet the definition of a covered entity or business associate, it does not have to comply with the HIPAA Rules." A patient sharing **their own** health information is not a HIPAA violation by the patient. Source: U.S. Dept. of Health & Human Services, *Covered Entities and Business Associates*, https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/index.html (definitions at 45 CFR 160.103). Other laws (privacy of *other* people, defamation, recording laws below) can still apply, so be careful what you share about staff or other patients.

**California Health & Safety Code § 1278.5 (whistleblower protection).** California policy encourages patients, staff and others to report unsafe patient care. A health facility may not discriminate or retaliate against a **patient** (or employee, staff member or other health care worker) because they presented a grievance, complaint or report to the facility, an accrediting body, or a government entity. A rebuttable presumption of retaliation can arise for discriminatory treatment of a patient within **180 days** after a grievance or complaint. Text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=1278.5 . Complaints about facilities can be filed with the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Licensing & Certification.

**Caution on recordings: California Penal Code § 632.** California generally requires the consent of **all parties** to record a *confidential communication* (one where a party reasonably expects not to be overheard or recorded). Secretly recording a private conversation can be a crime and can create civil liability, and the recording may be inadmissible. Before recording anything, **announce it and get consent**, or ask your attorney first. Text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632 . Do not destroy any recording you already have; tell your attorney about it.

Other practical tips:
- Ask every representative for their **name, title, department and who they work for**, and write it down. Ask for it in writing by email.
- Follow up important conversations with a short, polite email summary ("To confirm our conversation today...").
- Do not sign releases, waivers or settlement papers without an attorney.
