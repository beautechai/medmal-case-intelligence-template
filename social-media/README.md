# social-media/: risk review

> ## 🛑 DO NOT DELETE OR EDIT POSTS ONCE LITIGATION IS REASONABLY ANTICIPATED.
> **Deleting, editing, hiding, or "cleaning up" posts, photos, comments or messages after you anticipate a claim can be *spoliation of evidence*.** Courts can sanction it, instruct a jury to assume the deleted content hurt you, or worse. **Preserve everything** (download your account archive, take dated screenshots that show the URL), **then show it to your attorney** and follow their advice. Changing privacy settings or deactivating accounts should also be cleared with counsel first.

Defense lawyers routinely review plaintiffs' public posts for statements that contradict claimed injuries (travel, sports, lifting, "feeling great"), statements about fault, or comments about providers. This template helps you inventory and rank that risk **for your attorney**.

## How to use
1. Download your data archive from each platform (most have a "download your information" tool) and store it in `data/private/`.
2. Add one row per relevant post to `social_media_review.csv` (copy `social_media_review_TEMPLATE.csv`).
3. Rank risk 1 to 5:

| Rank | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Directly contradicts claimed injury/limits or admits fault | photos of strenuous activity during claimed disability; "my own fault" |
| 4 | Discusses the case, providers or settlement | naming staff, accusing providers, discussing legal strategy |
| 3 | Shows activity/mood that could be taken out of context | vacation photos, parties |
| 2 | Health updates consistent with your account | recovery updates |
| 1 | Unrelated | |

4. Mark `preserved` and `shown_to_counsel`. **Do not delete.**
5. Going forward: consider pausing posting about health, activities or the case, and do not discuss the case online or in messages (messages are discoverable too).
